Fine-tuning Llama-3 for a regulated domain

Adapted Llama-3-8B for medical-device documentation Q&A under HIPAA constraints. PEFT + DPO + structured eval. Customer reports 28% fewer escalations to humans.

2025-Q2 — 2025-Q4 · Under NDA · summary published with client approval

Constraints

  • All training and inference on customer-controlled infrastructure (no calls to hosted APIs).
  • Audit trail for every model output: prompt, retrieved context, model version, and human review status.
  • No fine-tuning data could leave the customer environment, ever.
  • Latency budget: 1.2s p95 for short answers on a single A100.

Approach

  • Base model: Llama-3-8B-Instruct. Larger would have blown the latency budget; smaller didn't have the comprehension margin.
  • Adapter training: LoRA (rank 16) on 11k curated SFT examples generated from internal SME annotations, deduplicated against the public Llama corpus.
  • Preference alignment: DPO on 2.4k human-rated pairs targeting "answers questions actually in scope, declines politely otherwise." The decline-rate was the key business metric.
  • Inference: vLLM with continuous batching; PagedAttention let us co-locate retrieval-time prefill and generation on one GPU.
  • Eval: A custom variant of the RAG Eval Harness with a deterministic NLI judge — necessary because the client couldn't send eval data to a hosted judge model.

What went well

The decline-when-uncertain behavior held up under adversarial probing — the model rejected paraphrased out-of-scope questions at 91% precision, vs. 47% for the base instruct model. That's the headline number for the customer.

What didn't

First pass on DPO over-suppressed: the model declined too aggressively, including in-scope edge cases. Re-balanced the preference data, added back ~400 "correctly answered tricky question" pairs, and the rate normalized. Cost us two weeks.
